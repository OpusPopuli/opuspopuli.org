# Philosophical foundation

**Status:** canonical. This document is the source of truth for the intellectual case behind Opus
Populi, for `opuspopuli.org`, for every state node, and for anything written under the Friday
Philosophy line. Where site copy and this document disagree, this document is right.

**This replaces the Chenoweth 3.5% framing.** See §5 for what was removed and why, so that nobody
re-adds it in good faith.

---

## 1. The argument in three moves

**Descriptive.** In any large polity, an organized few decide and the disorganized many do not.
This is not a claim about corruption or conspiracy; it is a claim about coordination costs. It is
also measurable. Under California Elections Code §9118, a petition signed by 10% of a county's
gubernatorial vote compels its board of supervisors to act. That is roughly 60 people in Alpine
County. A group that size already governs there, whether or not it is the people who live there.

**Normative.** Formal political equality is worth little when the knowledge required to use it is
distributed as unevenly as wealth is. This is not a complaint about apathy. It is a defect in the
fair value of political liberty, and on Rawls's account a just society is obliged to repair it
rather than merely declare the right and walk away.

**Remedial.** The response is not to hand government to experts, and not to exhort citizens to
care more. It is to change the conditions under which citizens act: lower the cost of knowing what
is on the ballot, who is paying for it, and what can be done about it, at the scale where a single
person's participation is not negligible.

That third move is the product. The first two are why it matters.

## 2. The canon

Each entry gives the claim as we use it. **Paraphrase only — no direct quotation of any of these
works in site copy.** Most are in copyright and the argument does not need their exact words.

### Descriptive: the organized minority

**Gaetano Mosca**, *The Ruling Class* (1896)
A minority governs not because it is wiser or better born, but because it is organized against a
disorganized majority. Numbers are not power until they are coordinated. This is the intellectual
backbone of the county map: the threshold is Mosca's thesis expressed in signatures.

**Robert Michels**, *Political Parties* (1911)
The iron law of oligarchy. Organization itself concentrates control, including in movements
founded expressly to prevent that. Cite Michels honestly — he is the hardest version of our own
finding, and he applies to us. See §4.

**E. E. Schattschneider**, *The Semisovereign People* (1960)
Power lies in controlling which alternatives are considered at all, and the chorus of organized
interests sings with an upper-class accent. Whoever frames the ballot has won most of the argument
before any vote is cast. This is the most operationally useful entry in the canon: it explains why
a ballot-explainer is a political intervention and not a public service.

**Alexis de Tocqueville**, *Democracy in America* (1835)
Local institutions are where citizens learn the practice of liberty. This justifies the county as
our unit of analysis, which is otherwise a design choice we would have to defend on our own.

### Normative: why unequal knowledge is a justice problem

**John Rawls**, *A Theory of Justice* (1971)
The political liberties must have *fair value*, not merely formal equality. A liberty that some
citizens lack the resources to exercise is a liberty in name only, and its worth must be
guaranteed rather than asserted. **This is the strongest entry we have** and should carry the most
weight in any long-form treatment. It converts the mission from an aspiration into a claim about
what justice requires.

**John Dewey**, *The Public and Its Problems* (1927)
The public is eclipsed, not absent. It cannot recognize itself or trace which decisions affect it.
The remedy lies in improving the conditions of inquiry and communication. Written as a direct
answer to Lippmann; the exchange is the founding argument of this field and it is still live.

**Jürgen Habermas**, *The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere* (1962)
Legitimacy arises from reasoned discourse among informed equals; as the public sphere is captured
by commercial and administrative interests, that discourse thins into publicity. *Optional in
short treatments — the weakest link in the chain for a general audience.*

**Thomas Paine**, *Common Sense* (1776)
Wrote in deliberately plain language because comprehensibility was itself the political act. The
pamphlet was simultaneously the argument and the distribution method. Paine is the tonal ancestor
of the whole project, not just a citation.

**Hannah Arendt**, *The Human Condition* (1958) / *On Revolution* (1963)
Politics is action taken in public among equals, and freedom is a practice rather than a
possession. Retained from the original foundation; pairs naturally with Tocqueville on why the
local scale is where this is actually available to people.

## 3. The objections, stated at full strength

A platform premised on better civic information must answer the argument that better civic
information changes nothing. Never present these weakened.

### Anthony Downs, *An Economic Theory of Democracy* (1957) — rational ignorance

A single vote decides essentially nothing, so the expected benefit of becoming informed is near
zero while the cost is real. Remaining ignorant is not a character failure; it is the correct
decision. On this account, publishing more information changes nobody's behavior, because cost was
never the binding constraint — pointlessness was.

**Our answer, and it is the best thing we have.** Downs's conclusion rests on a product of two
terms: the cost of knowing, multiplied by how little one's participation matters. The tool
attacks the first term. **The county scale attacks the second, and attacks it harder.** One
signature among the 5,074 that compel Nevada County's board is not a lottery ticket; it is
approximately one part in five thousand of a binding outcome, several orders of magnitude removed
from one vote among eleven million in a statewide race. Downs is right about the state. He is much
weaker about the county.

This is why the county threshold, and not a statewide target, is the organizing device of the
whole site. It is not a presentation choice. It is the load-bearing response to the strongest
objection.

**Correction, 2026-09-02.** This figure previously read 5,137. The correct number is 5,074.
Nevada County cast 26,655 votes for Newsom and 24,082 for Dahle on 8 November 2022, totalling
50,737; ten percent, rounded up as §9118 requires, is 5,074. Derived from the Secretary of State's
Statement of the Vote (`elections.cdn.sos.ca.gov/sov/2022-general/sov/19-governor.xlsx`). The
county totals in that file sum to 10,933,018, and five and eight percent of that sum reproduce
California's published statewide requirements of 546,651 and 874,641 exactly, which is the check
that the county figures are right. The provenance of 5,137 could not be established. Per §6, do not
restore it without a source.

### Walter Lippmann, *Public Opinion* (1922) / *The Phantom Public* (1925)

Ordinary citizens cannot know enough to govern a modern society; decisions should rest with those
who can. Dewey's reply relocated the problem from the citizen to the conditions the citizen acts
under, and that is our wager: that what presents as incapacity is substantially an access cost.

### Jason Brennan, *Against Democracy* (2016)

The contemporary Lippmann. Voters are ignorant and motivated-reasoning-prone, so political power
should be weighted toward competence. Worth engaging directly rather than dismissing — readers who
lean this way already know the book, and refusing to name it reads as evasion.

**Our position is falsifiable and must remain so.** If civic knowledge becomes genuinely cheap and
participation does not improve, Brennan gains ground and we should say so rather than move the
goalposts.

## 4. What we concede, in public

These belong on the site, not in an internal doc. Stating them is the credibility.

1. **Ten percent qualifies a measure; it does not pass one.** A majority at the ballot is a
   separate and much larger problem, and nothing on our map addresses it.
2. **Lowering the cost of knowing does not supply a motive for caring.** We have no evidence yet
   that it does.
3. **Michels applies to Opus Populi.** An organization built to disperse civic power will tend to
   concentrate it. This is why the code is open, the AI charter is public, and the governing
   structure is a foundation rather than a company. Those are mitigations, not immunity.
4. **A tool that makes organizing cheaper makes it cheaper for everyone**, including people whose
   aims we would find objectionable. That is what neutrality costs, and we think it is the right
   price.

## 5. Removed: the Chenoweth 3.5% framing

**What was there.** That no campaign engaging 3.5% of a population has failed, applied to
California as roughly 26,000 people, presented as an activation target and apportioned across
counties.

**Why it was removed.**

- **The arithmetic does not survive.** 26,000 of about 39 million Californians is 0.066%. For
  26,000 to be 3.5% of anything, the denominator would have to be about 743,000, and no California
  population of that size means anything. 3.5% of the state's population is roughly 1.37 million;
  of the 2022 gubernatorial vote, about 383,000. Every honest denominator lands two orders of
  magnitude away. Any reader with a calculator catches this, and funders and reporters have
  calculators.
- **The finding does not transfer.** Chenoweth and Stephan's data concerns campaigns for
  state-wide systemic change, overwhelmingly attempts to overthrow authoritarian governments. It
  is not a finding about policy change in liberal democracies. Extinction Rebellion took sustained
  academic criticism for exactly this over-extension.
- **Chenoweth herself has cautioned against this use**, describing the figure as a descriptive
  statistic drawn from a historical sample rather than a prescriptive threshold, and noting that
  reaching it without a broad constituency guarantees nothing. It has also been exceeded and
  failed — Brunei in 1962, Bahrain in 2011–14.
- **Apportioning it by county is a different claim entirely.** The 3.5% observation concerns one
  polity mobilizing against one government at a peak moment. Splitting it into 58 pieces is not a
  simplification of the research; nothing in the underlying dataset supports it.
- **The activities are not comparable.** Sustained peak-event mobilization against a regime is not
  the same act as creating an account on a civic information site, and treating them as equivalent
  is the part a skeptical reader finds insulting.

**What replaces it.** §9118 and the statewide 5%/8% signature requirements. Real thresholds, set
by statute, verifiable at every county elections office, and — crucially — small in exactly the
way the Chenoweth framing was reaching for, without needing to borrow anyone's research.

**If someone wants to reintroduce Chenoweth**, the only defensible home is a long-form essay with
the caveats stated in full, arguing about the research rather than deploying it as a number. It
does not go on a landing page, where it is a load-bearing claim with nothing under it.

## 6. House rules for using this document

- **Paraphrase; never quote.** Nearly all of these works are in copyright, and the argument does
  not depend on their wording.
- **Never invent a citation, a page number, or a figure.** If a claim cannot be sourced, cut it.
- **State opposing arguments at their strongest.** A weakened Downs is worse than no Downs.
- **Prefer the checkable number to the impressive one.** This is the rule the Chenoweth removal
  came from, and it is the one most likely to be violated again under deadline.
